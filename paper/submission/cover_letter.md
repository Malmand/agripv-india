We submit the manuscript "Yield-constrained agrivoltaic potential of India under current and mid-century climate" for consideration as a Research Paper in *Applied Energy*.

India plans much of its new solar capacity on or near farmland, and published national estimates of its agrivoltaic potential range from about 1.2 to 13.8 TW. These estimates screen land by suitability rules and assume a fixed power density; none of them simulates the crop under the panels, although rules in Germany, Japan and France define agrivoltaics by a minimum crop yield.

Our study addresses this gap. To our knowledge, it is the first national estimate of agrivoltaic potential in which the panel density allowed in each 0.25° cell is set by simulated crop yield: a two-dimensional light model of three array layouts is coupled with the DSSAT crop models for wheat, chickpea, pearl millet and groundnut, calibrated to observed state yields, and run on 2977 cells with five climate models. The potential is reported for relative-yield thresholds of 66%, 80% and 90%, for two nitrogen management cases, and for 2041-2060 under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5. We find that [X GWp] of capacity on cropland with slopes up to 5% meets the 80% threshold, that nitrogen supply changes the estimate by [X]%, and that mid-century climate changes it by [X]%, with the CO2 effect on legume yields reported separately.

The work fits the journal's scope on renewable energy resource assessment and the energy-food nexus, and it builds on recent *Applied Energy* studies that optimised agrivoltaic layouts at single sites. The light model is benchmarked against pvlib and against light measured below an agrivoltaic system in Jodhpur, and the limits of a light-only shading model are stated in the Discussion.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors approved the submission. The code and processed results will be released on Zenodo [DOI to be added]. We declare no competing interests.
