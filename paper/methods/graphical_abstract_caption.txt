Graphical abstract. Left: solar PV and crops compete for the same farmland. Centre: a light model and the DSSAT crop model give the relative yield (RY, crop yield under the array divided by open-field yield) as a function of the ground coverage ratio (GCR) for each cell, crop and layout; the curve is schematic. GCR_max is the largest GCR that keeps RY ≥ 0.8, found where the RY-GCR curve, linearly interpolated from RY = 1 at GCR 0, first falls below the threshold. Right: GCR_max at RY ≥ 0.8 for the east-west layout under 1995–2014 climate, robust case, in which each cell, crop and GCM takes the smaller GCR_max of the calibrated and recommended nitrogen runs. For each 0.25° cell and crop, GCR_max is first computed for each of five GCMs and the median of the five is taken. Eligible area is the MapSPAM harvested area of the crop times the share of the cell with terrain slope ≤ 5%. The map shows, per cell, the mean of the four crops' median GCR_max weighted by eligible area; cells with less than 1000 ha of eligible area are left blank. Each crop value is the mean of that crop's median GCR_max over all cells with eligible area, weighted by eligible area. Cereals were simulated up to GCR 0.7 and legumes up to 0.5, so cells that still meet the threshold at that limit enter at the limit. The headline is the crops-combined capacity (double-cropped land counted once, capacity = eligible area × GCR_max × 2.2 MWp ha^−1^ per unit GCR), summed nationally for each GCM; the value shown is the median of the five national totals. The badge gives the change in this capacity by 2041–2060 under SSP5-8.5 against 1995–2014 with calibrated nitrogen: the change of the five-GCM median totals, and the range of the change over the five GCMs. State and union-territory boundaries: DataMeet India community, States/Admin2 (https://github.com/datameet/maps, CC BY 4.0).
