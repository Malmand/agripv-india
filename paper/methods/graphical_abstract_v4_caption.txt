Graphical abstract. A light model for three array layouts and the DSSAT crop model for wheat, chickpea, pearl millet and groundnut give, for every 0.25° cell, the largest panel cover (ground coverage ratio, the share of land covered by modules) that keeps at least 80% of the open-field yield. The map shows it for east-west elevated arrays under 1995–2014 climate, robust case, in which each cell, crop and GCM takes the smaller panel cover of the calibrated and recommended nitrogen runs: the median over five GCMs, averaged over the four crops with eligible-area weights (MapSPAM harvested area on the share of the cell with slope ≤ 5%); cells with less than 1000 ha are blank, the top of the scale (70%) is the largest cover simulated, and the 0.25° values are interpolated for display. The map is the crop-model (upper) case. Capacity is the crops-combined total with double-cropped land counted once (median of five GCMs); the lower end of the range rescales the simulated relative yield to field observations of yield under reduced light, and the upper end is the crop-model case. Cereal and legume values are eligible-area-weighted means; the call-outs give the share of the field covered by panels, the same mean, for the wheat belt (24–31°N, 74–88°E) and the Deccan (12–21°N, 73–80°E); The 2050 values are the changes by 2041–2060 under SSP5-8.5 against 1995–2014 (five-GCM median, calibrated nitrogen). State and union-territory boundaries: DataMeet India community, States/Admin2 (https://github.com/datameet/maps, CC BY 4.0). The curves are the area-weighted national mean relative yield of wheat and chickpea against the share of land under panels (east-west layout, recommended nitrogen, 1995–2014); the panel limit is where a curve crosses 80%. GCMs, global climate models.
